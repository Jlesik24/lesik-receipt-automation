1. What This App Is

Opportunity Communications is a private tool used by one operator. The operator uses it to publish posts to Facebook Pages that the operator administers. It is part of the operator's internal business software. The internal software prepares posts and is designed to publish them only on the operator's instruction or approval.

The app has no public application interface and no public sign-up. Its privacy and deletion information is published on this public website. Only the operator authorizes Facebook access, through a manual setup process; the app does not offer "Log in with Facebook" to anyone.

2. Facebook Pages the App Manages

  • Skyview Amusements Pool Leagues
  • Last Rack Billiards

3. Permissions the App Uses

The table lists the permissions that the app's setup and publishing code is written to use.

pages_show_list During setup, to list the Pages returned for the operator's account so the operator can choose the two Pages above.
pages_manage_posts To publish text posts to one of the two Pages. Photo publishing is not currently available through the software.

4. What the App Reads From Facebook

The app is designed to read the following:

  • Before each publication: the ID and name of the Page being published to.
  • Before each publication, the app reads up to ten of the Page's most recent published posts, including posts made outside this app, to establish a baseline. It may read them again to reconcile an uncertain publication result. Returned post IDs, text and creation times are compared in memory; the returned list is not retained, although the identified published post ID is recorded.
  • After a publication: the ID that Facebook returns for the new post.
  • During setup, a script run by the operator retrieves the IDs, names, tasks and available access tokens for Pages returned for the operator's account. It stores configuration for the two selected Pages. The app secret and user access tokens are processed during setup but are not stored.
  • After a post is created, the software is designed to check whether a logged-out visitor can see it. It requests the post's public Facebook web pages without logging in and without sending any access token, shortly after publication and again over the following 24 hours; its schedule is configured for at most twelve automatic requests per post. The operator can also run such a check on request. The software keeps the result of each check and a short note of what was found; it is designed not to keep the fetched pages.

5. What the App Does Not Request

The app does not specifically request Facebook user profiles, visitor identities, follower lists, comments or reactions. Page post text may contain people's names, and league records used to prepare posts may also contain personal information, as explained in section 6.

The software contains no requests for any of the following:

  • Email addresses, friend lists or other account details of Facebook users.
  • Comments, reactions or shares on posts.
  • Page insights or other audience statistics.
  • Messenger conversations, Instagram accounts, Groups or advertising accounts.

The software has no webhook or other endpoint for receiving notifications from Meta.

6. Content of Posts and People Named in Them

The posts the app publishes are written by or for the operator. Pool league result posts can include the names of league players and teams together with their match results and achievements. That information originates from the leagues' records in CompuSport, a league-management service, and not from Facebook. Once such a post is published, its text is part of the Page's posts and can be read back from Facebook as described in section 4.

If you are named in a post, draft or image and want it corrected or removed, contact the operator using the details in section 13. See the Data Deletion Instructions. Requests about other records the business holds, such as league records, are handled separately by the operator.

7. How Posts Are Approved

The software is designed to publish to Facebook only when the operator has either given an explicit instruction containing the exact text and the specific Page, or approved the exact draft shown for approval. An approval is tied to that exact text (and image, if there is one) and that one Page; the software is designed to check this again immediately before publishing and to refuse if either has changed.

8. What Is Stored

The app maintains the records below on the operator's computer. Additional copies and disclosures are described in section 9 (backup copies) and section 10 (sharing and service providers).

  • The two Page IDs, the two Page names, and one Page access token for each Page, in a configuration file. The Page IDs are also held in the software's database.
  • A delivery log entry for each post, until eligible cleanup (section 9): which Page, the time, the status, the Facebook post ID, a fingerprint (hash) of the content, its length, and the first 120 characters of the post text.
  • A record of each approval: identifiers, fingerprints (hashes), when it was given, the outcome, the Facebook post ID, and any remaining preview of the post text. A short excerpt of that preview is also kept in the software's internal work list.
  • The full text of a draft while it waits for approval. Text in the communication draft record is designed to be cleared when the draft is sent or cancelled, or when cleanup processes an expired draft. Drafts are set to expire after 24 hours. Other copies, including weekly post versions, approval previews and Telegram messages, have separate retention.
  • For weekly league result posts: the full text of each version of the post and the time it was published.
  • If a post includes an image: the image file, in a folder on the same computer (no post has included an image to date).
  • For the logged-out visibility check: for each post, the Page ID and post ID, a copy of the post text used for comparison, the check status and times, and for each check its result, method and a short description of what was found.
  • The software also retains delivery diagnostics, approval and workflow history, league schedule information, and redacted command records.
  • If you send a privacy or deletion request: your message and contact details, in the operator's email mailbox. No request log exists yet. A proposed deletion procedure, which is not yet approved or in use, would create a request log and entries identifying changed posts, including status markers and verification fingerprints (keyed hashes of sample passages of removed text), without retaining the removed text itself.

9. How Long Information Is Kept

  • Eligible delivery log entries older than 90 days are designed to be removed when the software starts. Entries can remain longer if the software has not restarted. This cleanup does not remove approval records, weekly post text or backup copies.
  • Approval records, weekly post text, visibility-check records, workflow history and league schedule information have no automatic deletion schedule.
  • Image files and operational logs have no automatic deletion schedule.
  • Stored Page configuration has no automatic deletion schedule. Disconnecting the app does not itself erase local records or backup copies.
  • Request emails have no automatic deletion schedule. No request log is currently in use.
  • If the operator stops using the app with a Page, the app's access is removed, the User requests deletion of information obtained from Facebook, or Meta requires its deletion (which for this app includes the two Page access tokens, the Page IDs and names, the Facebook post IDs, and delivery-status and visibility-check details obtained from Facebook), the operator will promptly end the app's access to the affected Page(s) on Facebook, which is intended to make the stored Page access tokens unusable, and will promptly delete the Page access tokens, Page IDs and Page names from the software's active configuration and from local copies of that configuration. The remaining copies - Page IDs, Facebook post IDs and those details held in the software's database and in notices in the operator's private Telegram chat, copies of them in local safety copies of the database, and copies of the configuration and the database in off-site backups - are not removed by those steps; the operator removes them in a separate step as soon as reasonably possible and does not use them after access is removed. Deleting the Telegram notices is available by hand today. Removal from the database, its copies and off-site backups requires supporting tools that have been tested on synthetic data; that work has not yet been rehearsed or verified in production. The backup item below describes those copies. The 30-calendar-day completion target in the Data Deletion Instructions does not limit or delay this paragraph.
  • Backup copies: the computer is configured to be backed up nightly to off-site storage. Backup snapshot selection uses 7 daily, 4 weekly and 6 monthly retention settings. These settings do not guarantee erasure within six months. Data from removed snapshots remains in storage until a separate cleanup is run; that cleanup has not yet been run. The operator also keeps local safety copies of database and configuration files made before software changes. Local safety copies have no scheduled deletion and are included in subsequent off-site backups. Backup copies made before a removal, and later backups containing older local safety copies, may retain removed information.
  • The backup and safety-copy descriptions in this section apply except as stated above for information obtained from Facebook.
  • Posts published to a Page remain on Facebook unless they are removed there.

To ask for something to be corrected or deleted, see the Data Deletion Instructions.

10. Sharing and Service Providers

The operator uses information obtained from Facebook to publish and keep track of the Page posts described above. The operator does not sell it, does not use it for advertising or to build profiles of people, and does not provide it to data brokers. The software's Facebook component is designed not to pass information received from Facebook to any artificial intelligence service.

The following service providers are involved:

  • Meta (Facebook): receives the posts the operator publishes and the requests the app makes, over HTTPS. This includes the logged-out visibility checks, which are ordinary requests to Facebook's public website.
  • Telegram: the operator receives draft posts and approval requests, and gives approvals, in the operator's private Telegram chat with the software. Draft text and approval previews are therefore sent through Telegram and remain in that chat unless deleted. Notices about a published post, which include its Facebook post ID, are also sent to that chat.
  • Google Drive: stores the nightly off-site backup of the operator's computer, which includes the records and configuration described in section 8, including Page access tokens. The backup is configured to be encrypted on the operator's computer before it is uploaded.
  • CompuSport: the source of league results used to prepare posts. The app does not send Facebook information to it.
  • Email provider: requests sent to the contact address in section 13 are received in a mailbox at the provider of that address (outlook.com, a Microsoft service).
  • GitHub Pages: hosts these policy pages; the app does not upload its operational database, credentials or draft records to GitHub Pages.

Information may also be disclosed when required by law.

11. Security

  • The software runs on the operator's computer and is intended for use on the operator's private network. Not every connection to its interface on that network is encrypted.
  • The local interface is configured to require an administrator password, which is stored in hashed form. The internal software uses a session cookie for administrator authentication; sessions are set to expire.
  • Telegram approvals are designed to be accepted only from the configured operator account in a private chat.
  • Local database and configuration files, including local safety copies, are not separately encrypted and are not restricted to a single user account on that computer. The software is designed to send Page tokens only to Facebook, over HTTPS, for API authentication; encrypted backup copies containing tokens are also stored on Google Drive.
  • Off-site backup copies are configured to be encrypted before they leave the operator's computer.

This policy page contains no analytics scripts or tracking code added by the operator.

12. Children and Participants Under 18

The app is an operator-only business tool, not a service offered to children. League and event post content may contain personal information about participants.

  • Skyview Pool Leagues is open only to players aged 21 and over.
  • Last Rack Billiards tournaments and events are open to players of all ages, including players under 18. Results posted to the Last Rack Billiards Page may therefore name participants who are under 18.

A participant, or a parent or guardian of a participant under 18, may ask for correction or removal using the contact address in section 13.
